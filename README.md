# Learn Arabic — Vocabulary Game

A simple, offline flashcard + quiz game for kids learning Arabic vocabulary.
Built for a touchscreen (like your Surface) — big tap targets, no typing
required once a player's name is saved.

## How to run it

1. Copy the whole `arabic-vocab-game` folder to your Surface (keep
   `index.html`, `words.js`, and `letters.js` together, in the same folder).
2. Double-click `index.html`. It opens in your default browser
   (Edge works great on a Surface).
3. That's it — no install, no server needed.

**One important habit:** always open the game from that same folder/file.
Each player's points and progress are saved by the browser to that exact
file location, so if you move or rename `index.html` later, past progress
won't carry over automatically (you'd just start fresh players).

### Pronunciation

Everything spoken by the app is played from pre-downloaded audio files in
the `audio/` folder — two per word, named by its `id`:

- `audio/w001.mp3` — the Arabic word
- `audio/w001_en.mp3` — its English meaning

Letters (from `letters.js`) work the same way, just one file each (there's
no English translation to speak for a letter) — `audio/l01.mp3` is the
first letter's name, and so on. Word ids (`wNNN`) and letter ids (`lNN`)
never collide, so both live in the same `audio/` folder.

Both are the Google Translate voice, fetched once ahead of time, so
playback is instant and the app works **fully offline**. Keeping the
English local matters as much as the Arabic: Google's live TTS endpoint is
blocked from some origins (GitHub Pages among them), and without a local
file the English drops to the much flatter built-in Windows voice.

Behind the scenes, each clip is generated by asking Google for "word,
word" (twice) and trimming down to just the second repetition — Google's
TTS endpoint has a known bug where it clips the very start of short audio
clips, and the second repetition is never the one at risk. That trimming
step needs `ffmpeg`; `generate_audio.sh` uses it automatically if it's on
your PATH, and just keeps both repetitions (still correct, just says the
word twice) if it isn't installed.

If a word's audio file is ever missing, the game falls back to fetching
Google's live voice over the internet, and if that also fails it falls
back again to your browser/Windows' built-in voice — noticeably flatter,
but it keeps working. A couple of notes:

- Every card also has a 🔊 speaker button so a player can replay the word
  anytime, in case autoplay didn't fire.
- Make sure your Surface's volume is up and the browser tab isn't muted.
- For the built-in-voice fallback to sound decent, Windows may still need
  an Arabic speech voice installed: **Settings → Time & Language →
  Language & region → Add a language → Arabic**, with "Speech" included.

## How the game works

- **Player select** — each kid types their name once; after that, they just
  tap their name/avatar to jump back in. Points and progress are saved per
  player, right in the browser.
- After picking a player, choose which game to play: **Arabic Vocab**
  (below) or **Arabic Alphabet** (see its own section further down). Switch
  between them anytime from a "🎮 Switch" button on either game's home
  screen — no need to re-pick your name.
- The vocab game's home screen is organized into four sections: **Learn**,
  **Practice**, **Quiz**, and **Review**.

### Learn

- **Listen & Learn** — teaches a small batch of words (5 by default,
  adjustable) in three passes, then hands off to a quiz on those same
  words. See "How Listen & Learn works" below.
- **Handwriting** — trace the Arabic word on screen with a finger (or
  mouse) to practice writing it.

### Practice

- **Flashcards: Arabic → English** — shows the Arabic word (and says it out
  loud), tap the card to flip and see the English meaning.
- **Flashcards: English → Arabic** — shows the English word, tap to flip and
  reveal (and hear) the Arabic word.
- **Flashcards: Mixed** — the same flashcards, but each card randomly picks
  a direction (Arabic→English or English→Arabic), for extra challenge.

### Quiz

- **Listen & Choose: Arabic → English** — the Arabic word is spoken aloud,
  and the player taps the matching English word out of four choices.
- **Listen & Choose: English → Arabic** — the English word is shown and
  spoken, and the player taps the matching Arabic word out of four choices.

### Review

- **See My Progress** — every active word, weakest-scoring first, with a
  color-coded accuracy meter and a 14-day accuracy trend chart. Never-
  attempted words show up last, labeled "Not started yet" instead of a
  score.
- **Practice My Tricky Words** — a flashcard round using only the words a
  player is currently scoring 70% or under on (after at least two
  attempts). Nothing to practice yet, and it says so instead of starting
  an empty round.

### Word lists

Words are grouped into **lists** — right now, List 1 (the original 20
words: names, phrases, classroom objects), List 2 (15 words: everyday
phrases, classroom supplies, and "my mother"), List 3 (15 words: the
rest of the family members, plus personality traits), and List 4 (14
words: the pillars of Islam, plus more character traits). Every player
picks which list(s) they want to practice — right after choosing their
name, and any time afterward from the **📚 Word lists** button on the
home screen.
Every mode (flashcards, quizzes, Handwriting, Listen & Learn, Tricky Words)
only draws from whichever list(s) are currently selected.

## Arabic Alphabet game

A second, separate game for learning the 28 letters of the Arabic
alphabet — pick it instead of Arabic Vocab right after choosing a player.
It has its own four modes:

- **Pick the Letter** — the whole alphabet is shown on screen, in order,
  greyed out. The narrator says a letter; tap it. Get it right and it
  fills in with a sparkly sound; get it wrong and it fills in a different
  color with a "Try again!" prompt so you can keep guessing. Every attempt
  feeds Tricky Letters below.
- **Flashcards** — a big letter card; tap it to hear it pronounced (as
  many times as you like), then say whether you knew it with the same
  "Still learning" / "I knew it!" buttons as the vocab flashcards.
- **Tracing** — a big, faint letter is shown; color over it with a finger
  or mouse. Just practice, no grading and no points, same idea as vocab's
  Handwriting mode.
- **Write!** — the narrator says a letter and the player writes it from
  memory in a blank box, with a button to save that attempt and move to
  the next letter. Nothing is graded in the moment — see Parent Review.

### Review (Alphabet game)

- **Tricky Letters** — a simple list of letters currently scoring 70% or
  under (after at least two attempts), same idea as the vocab game's
  tricky-words list.
- **Parent Review** — every saved Write! attempt, newest first, showing
  the target letter next to what the player actually drew. Mark each one
  ✓ Correct or ✗ Incorrect; correct ones award 10 points and both feed
  Tricky Letters. Graded attempts are removed from the list immediately
  (nothing to re-review), and at most the most recent 60 ungraded
  attempts are kept per player so this can't grow without bound.

### How Listen & Learn works

Rather than reading the whole list top-to-bottom, it takes a **small batch
of words** (5 by default — use the ＋/－ buttons to change it) and mixes
two kinds of step together:

- **👂 New word** — hear the Arabic, hear the English, then the banner
  turns gold for a couple of seconds of silence. That's the child's cue to
  *say the word out loud*, and then it plays once more so they can check
  themselves.
- **🤔 Do you remember?** — hear the Arabic while that word's meaning is
  covered up (`? ? ?`), a few seconds to remember it, then the answer is
  revealed and read aloud.

Each word is tested **three times, at growing gaps**. The first test comes
almost straight after the word is taught, when it's still fresh and the
child will almost certainly get it; later tests are pushed further and
further back, with other words filling the space in between. A meaning
stays covered on screen until that word has actually been taught, so the
list can't be read ahead.

At the end of the batch it offers to **quiz those exact words**, practice
them again, or move on to the next batch.

The reason for all this: repeating a word five times in a row makes it
*feel* familiar without making it stick. What builds memory is recalling
the word from memory, and spacing those recalls out. But retrieval only
helps while the child is mostly succeeding — a test they reliably fail is
just discouraging. Starting the first test almost immediately and then
stretching the gap keeps it effortful but achievable.

A batch of 5 takes roughly 2 minutes. Short and frequent beats long and
occasional, so a couple of batches a day is plenty.

### Review scheduling (spaced repetition)

Every word quietly tracks a "box" level and a next-due date. Answer a word
correctly and it moves up a box and won't come back around for longer
(1 day → 2 → 4 → 8 → 16 → 30). Get it wrong and it drops back to box 0 and
returns in the very next round. The home screen shows how many words are
ready for review, and every mode front-loads whatever is due.

This is what turns short daily sessions into long-term memory — a few
minutes a day beats one long cram session, and the app handles deciding
what to show when.

### Points and word coverage

- **Points** — 10 points for a correct quiz answer, 5 points for a
  flashcard a player marks "I knew it!" Points show live in the header.
- Practice and Quiz rounds cover every word in the player's selected
  list(s) each time, ordered so that words due for review come first and
  weaker words surface earlier — nothing gets left out, but the most
  useful practice happens first if the session gets cut short.
  Handwriting practice uses a smaller 10-word subset per round.

## Adding more words later

Everything you need is in **`words.js`** — open it in any text editor
(Notepad is fine). It's a plain list like this:

```js
{ id: "w051", arabic: "شَجَرَة", transliteration: "shajara", english: "tree", category: "nature", list: "starter" },
```

- `id` — must be unique across every word, in every list; just keep
  counting up (the highest so far is w050).
- `arabic` — the word itself. Adding the small vowel marks (tashkeel) helps
  the pronunciation come out more accurate, but it's optional.
- `transliteration` — a plain-English sounding-out of the word, shown only
  as a small hint on the English→Arabic flashcard back (not used in quizzes).
- `english` — the translation shown/quizzed on.
- `category` — a short label (e.g. `animals`, `colors`, `nature`). It's used
  to pick sensible wrong answers in the multiple-choice game. Reuse an
  existing category, or make up a new one — nothing else needs to change.
- `list` — which list (by id, e.g. `"starter"` or `"home-family"`) this
  word belongs to. To add a whole new list, add an entry to `WORD_LISTS`
  near the top of `words.js` with a new id and display name, then use
  that id here.

Save the file, refresh the page in the browser, and the new words are in
the game immediately — no restart, no rebuild.

New words won't have local audio files yet, so they'll fall back to the
built-in Windows voice and sound noticeably worse than the rest. To
generate their `audio/<id>.mp3` and `audio/<id>_en.mp3` files, open a
terminal in this folder and run `generate_audio.sh` (double-click works
too if `.sh` files are set to open with Git Bash) — it only downloads
audio for words that don't already have a file, so it's safe to re-run
anytime. **Do this before handing the app to the kids**, otherwise the
new words will sound out of place next to the old ones.

## Notes

- Everything is stored locally in the browser (no internet account, no data
  leaves the device) — it just means progress is tied to this browser on
  this computer.
- If you ever want to reset a player's stats, the simplest way is to open
  the browser's developer tools (F12) → Application/Storage tab → Local
  Storage → delete the `arabicVocabGame_v1` entry. That clears **all**
  players, so only do this if you mean to start everyone over.
