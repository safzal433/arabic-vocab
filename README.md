# Learn Arabic — Vocabulary Game

A simple, offline flashcard + quiz game for kids learning Arabic vocabulary.
Built for a touchscreen (like your Surface) — big tap targets, no typing
required once a player's name is saved.

## How to run it

1. Copy the whole `arabic-vocab-game` folder to your Surface (keep
   `index.html` and `words.js` together, in the same folder).
2. Double-click `index.html`. It opens in your default browser
   (Edge works great on a Surface).
3. That's it — no install, no server needed.

**One important habit:** always open the game from that same folder/file.
Each player's points and progress are saved by the browser to that exact
file location, so if you move or rename `index.html` later, past progress
won't carry over automatically (you'd just start fresh players).

### Arabic pronunciation

Pronunciation is played from pre-downloaded audio files in the `audio/`
folder (one mp3 per word, named by its `id`, e.g. `audio/w001.mp3`). These
are the Google Translate Arabic voice, fetched once ahead of time — so
playback is instant and works **fully offline**.

Behind the scenes, each clip is generated by asking Google for "word,
word" (twice) and trimming down to just the second repetition — Google's
TTS endpoint has a known bug where it clips the very start of short audio
clips, and the second repetition is never the one at risk. That trimming
step needs `ffmpeg`; `generate_audio.sh` uses it automatically if it's on
your PATH, and just keeps both repetitions (still correct, just says the
word twice) if it isn't installed.

If a word's audio file is ever missing, the game automatically falls back
to fetching Google Translate's live voice over the internet, and if that
also fails (offline, endpoint blocked), it falls back again to your
browser/Windows' built-in text-to-speech voice — lower quality, but it
still works offline. A couple of notes:

- Every card also has a 🔊 speaker button so a player can replay the word
  anytime, in case autoplay didn't fire.
- Make sure your Surface's volume is up and the browser tab isn't muted.
- For the built-in-voice fallback to sound decent, Windows may still need
  an Arabic speech voice installed: **Settings → Time & Language →
  Language & region → Add a language → Arabic**, with "Speech" included.

## How the game works

- **Player select** — each kid types their name once; after that, they just
  tap their name/avatar to jump back in. Points and progress are saved per
  player, right in the browser.
- The home screen is organized into four sections: **Learn**, **Practice**,
  **Quiz**, and **Review**.

### Learn

- **Listen & Learn** — teaches a small batch of words (5 by default,
  adjustable) in three passes, then hands off to a quiz on those same
  words. See "How Listen & Learn works" below.
- **Handwriting** — trace the Arabic word on screen with a finger (or
  mouse) to practice writing it.

### Practice

- **Flashcards: Arabic → English** — shows the Arabic word (and says it out
  loud), tap the card to flip and see the English meaning.
- **Flashcards: English → Arabic** — shows the English word, tap to flip and
  reveal (and hear) the Arabic word.
- **Flashcards: Mixed** — the same flashcards, but each card randomly picks
  a direction (Arabic→English or English→Arabic), for extra challenge.

### Quiz

- **Listen & Choose: Arabic → English** — the Arabic word is spoken aloud,
  and the player taps the matching English word out of four choices.
- **Listen & Choose: English → Arabic** — the English word is shown and
  spoken, and the player taps the matching Arabic word out of four choices.

### Review

- **See My Tricky Words** — pulls together whichever words a player has
  gotten wrong most often (after they've seen a word at least twice), so
  you can see at a glance what needs more practice.
- **Practice My Tricky Words** — a flashcard round using only the words a
  player is currently scoring 70% or under on (after at least two
  attempts). Nothing to practice yet, and it says so instead of starting
  an empty round.

### Word lists

Words are grouped into **lists** — right now, "Names & Classroom" (the
original 20 words) and "Family & Character" (30 more: family members,
personality traits, and a few more classroom supplies). Every player picks
which list(s) they want to practice — right after choosing their name, and
any time afterward from the **📚 Word lists** button on the home screen.
Every mode (flashcards, quizzes, Handwriting, Listen & Learn, Tricky Words)
only draws from whichever list(s) are currently selected.

### How Listen & Learn works

Rather than reading the whole list top-to-bottom, it takes a **small batch
of words** (5 by default — use the ＋/－ buttons to change it) and makes
three passes over that batch:

1. **👂 Listen** — hear the Arabic word, then its English meaning.
2. **🗣️ Your turn** — hear the Arabic, then the banner turns gold for a
   couple of seconds of silence. That's the child's cue to *say the word
   out loud*, and then it plays again so they can check themselves.
3. **🤔 Do you remember?** — hear the Arabic while the meanings on screen
   are covered up (`? ? ?`), a few seconds to think, then the answer is
   revealed and read aloud.

At the end of the batch it offers to **quiz those exact words**, practice
them again, or move on to the next batch.

This is deliberately different from just repeating each word five times in
a row. Repeating a word back-to-back makes it feel familiar without making
it stick — the two things that actually build memory are *spacing* the
repeats out and *actively recalling* the word instead of just re-hearing
it. The echo and recall passes are where the real learning happens, so
it's worth letting the silences play out rather than skipping ahead.

### Review scheduling (spaced repetition)

Every word quietly tracks a "box" level and a next-due date. Answer a word
correctly and it moves up a box and won't come back around for longer
(1 day → 2 → 4 → 8 → 16 → 30). Get it wrong and it drops back to box 0 and
returns in the very next round. The home screen shows how many words are
ready for review, and every mode front-loads whatever is due.

This is what turns short daily sessions into long-term memory — a few
minutes a day beats one long cram session, and the app handles deciding
what to show when.

### Points and word coverage

- **Points** — 10 points for a correct quiz answer, 5 points for a
  flashcard a player marks "I knew it!" Points show live in the header.
- Practice and Quiz rounds cover every word in the player's selected
  list(s) each time, ordered so that words due for review come first and
  weaker words surface earlier — nothing gets left out, but the most
  useful practice happens first if the session gets cut short.
  Handwriting practice uses a smaller 10-word subset per round.

## Adding more words later

Everything you need is in **`words.js`** — open it in any text editor
(Notepad is fine). It's a plain list like this:

```js
{ id: "w051", arabic: "شَجَرَة", transliteration: "shajara", english: "tree", category: "nature", list: "starter" },
```

- `id` — must be unique across every word, in every list; just keep
  counting up (the highest so far is w050).
- `arabic` — the word itself. Adding the small vowel marks (tashkeel) helps
  the pronunciation come out more accurate, but it's optional.
- `transliteration` — a plain-English sounding-out of the word, shown only
  as a small hint on the English→Arabic flashcard back (not used in quizzes).
- `english` — the translation shown/quizzed on.
- `category` — a short label (e.g. `animals`, `colors`, `nature`). It's used
  to pick sensible wrong answers in the multiple-choice game. Reuse an
  existing category, or make up a new one — nothing else needs to change.
- `list` — which list (by id, e.g. `"starter"` or `"home-family"`) this
  word belongs to. To add a whole new list, add an entry to `WORD_LISTS`
  near the top of `words.js` with a new id and display name, then use
  that id here.

Save the file, refresh the page in the browser, and the new words are in
the game immediately — no restart, no rebuild.

New words won't have a local audio file yet, so they'll sound slightly
slower at first (falling back to the live internet voice). To generate
their `audio/<id>.mp3` file so they're instant and offline like the rest,
open a terminal in this folder and run `generate_audio.sh` (double-click
works too if `.sh` files are set to open with Git Bash) — it only
downloads audio for words that don't already have a file, so it's safe to
re-run anytime.

## Notes

- Everything is stored locally in the browser (no internet account, no data
  leaves the device) — it just means progress is tied to this browser on
  this computer.
- If you ever want to reset a player's stats, the simplest way is to open
  the browser's developer tools (F12) → Application/Storage tab → Local
  Storage → delete the `arabicVocabGame_v1` entry. That clears **all**
  players, so only do this if you mean to start everyone over.
